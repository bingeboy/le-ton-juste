# Tube Inventory & Recommendations — Alembic FX-1

The Alembic FX-1 is a Fender Showman-derived tube preamp. Tube choice significantly affects the preamp's character, noise floor, and headroom.

## Current Inventory

### NOS GE 5751 5 Star Gray Plate 3 Mica

The **classic "baller" tube** for Jerry tones.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | 5751 (low-gain dual triode, 12AX7 substitute) |
| Gain Factor | ~70 (vs. 100 for 12AX7) |
| Construction | Gray plate, 3 mica spacers, 5 Star rating (GE's highest QA grade) |
| Era | 1950s–1960s NOS |
| Character | Smooth, articulate, lower noise than 12AX7 |

**Why it works:** The lower gain (70 vs 100) means more headroom before clipping. The gray plate and 3-mica construction yields a smoother, more musical harmonic structure. Jerry's clean tone was all about headroom and articulation — the 5751 delivers.

### Gain Factor Comparison

```
12AX7 (100) — full gain, most compression, earliest breakup
5751 (70)  — ~30% less gain, more headroom, cleaner
12AT7 (60) — even less gain, often used for reverb drivers (not typically a preamp V1 tube)
12AU7 (20) — very low gain, for specific uses (effects loop buffers, phase inverters)
```

For the Jerry clean tone: **5751 is the sweet spot.** Enough gain to drive the tube stage into its linear range without pushing into audible clipping. The "edge of breakup" is further away, leaving more room for dynamics.

## Positions

If the Alembic FX-1 uses two tubes (V1 = input stage, V2 = output/driver stage):

- **V1 (input):** The 5751 here has the biggest tonal impact. This is the first gain stage the guitar signal hits. Lower noise tubes are most important here.
- **V2 (output/driver):** A 12AX7 or 5751. Less impact on noise floor, more on overall gain structure.

## Tube Maintenance

- Preamp tubes in a rack unit typically last 5,000–10,000 hours
- Unlike power tubes, preamp tubes don't wear out quickly — they get microphonic or noisy
- Signs of a failing preamp tube: hiss, crackle, intermittent signal, microphonic ringing
- Always have a spare JJ ECC83S as an emergency backup
- Let tubes warm up for 2–3 minutes before playing for best tone
- Keep the rack well-ventilated — tubes generate heat
