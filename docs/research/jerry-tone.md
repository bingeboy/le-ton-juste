# Jerry Garcia — Early 1970s Clean Tone

## Signal Chain (1973–1974, Wall of Sound Era)

```
Doug Irwin / Alembic Custom Guitar ("Wolf")
  → Onboard Alembic buffer/preamp (active electronics)
  → Effects Send → Wah → Distortion → Mu-Tron III → Effects Return
  → Onboard Master Volume
  → Alembic F-2B Tube Preamp (1U rack, dual 12AX7, Fender Showman topology)
  → Fender Reverb Unit (standalone 6G15-type tube-driven spring reverb)
  → McIntosh MC2300 Power Amp (300W/ch solid state, "Budman")
  → Column of 20x JBL 12" speakers (D120F or E120)
```

## Key Takeaways for Our Rig

### 1. The Preamp IS the Amp

The F-2B replaced the need for a traditional guitar amplifier head entirely. It provided Fender-style tube warmth and EQ, while the McIntosh power amp added only clean volume. Our Alembic FX-1 serves the same role.

### 2. Reverb Between Preamp and Power Amp

Jerry's Fender Reverb Unit sat after the F-2B and before the MC2300. This is the canonical placement — spring reverb driven by the already-EQ'd preamp signal, amplified cleanly by the power amp. Our custom reverb tank should follow this same placement.

### 3. Massive, Clean Headroom

The MC2300 (300W/ch) into 20 JBLs meant Jerry could play at stadium volumes with ZERO power amp clipping. Everything stayed pristine. Our MC100 (100W into 2 JBLs) provides the same philosophy at smaller scale — more than enough headroom to never clip.

### 4. JBL = The Voice

Aluminum dome dust caps, wide frequency response, no speaker breakup. The speakers contribute as much to the "Jerry sound" as the preamp. Our E120-8s are the direct descendant of his D120F/K120/E120 columns.

### 5. OBEL = Consistent Dynamics

The onboard buffer + effects loop meant Jerry could ride his volume knob without changing how hard he hit his pedals. Volume was always post-effects. This is something to work toward with a pedal-form OBEL or future guitar mod.

## What NOT to Copy

- Jerry used a solid-body custom guitar (Wolf) with humbuckers, not a Strat (he moved on from Alligator by late '73)
- The Wall of Sound used 48 McIntosh MC2300s total — for one guitar, one channel of one MC2300 was used
- Jerry's tone got progressively cleaner across the 70s — early '72 (Europe) still had some Twin Reverb amp grit; by '74 (Wall of Sound) it was completely pristine
- The Fender Reverb Unit (6G15) is a tube-driven unit — it adds its own subtle harmonic coloration. Our custom reverb tank should consider whether we want tube or solid-state driver/recovery stages.

## Tonal Characteristics

- **Bright, articulate, glassy** — JBL aluminum domes + Fender Bright switch + clean power amp
- **Scooped mids** — Fender preamp topology with a natural mid-scoop
- **Fast transient attack** — Clean power amp, no compression/sag
- **Tube warmth without saturation** — The F-2B ran clean; it wasn't pushed into overdrive
- **Wide frequency range** — More like a PA than a guitar rig, intentionally

## Key Gear References

- **Alembic F-2B:** 1U, 2-channel, dual 12AX7, Bright switch, XLR + 1/4" out
- **McIntosh MC2300 "Budman":** Jerry's personal unit, used continuously 1973–1993, longest-used piece of Dead gear
- **JBL D120F / E120:** Aluminum dome, 4" voice coil, 100–150W, very efficient
- **Fender Reverb Unit (6G15):** Tube driver (12AT7), tube recovery (12AX7), spring tank
- **Doug Irwin "Wolf":** First Irwin custom, onboard Alembic electronics, OBEL, humbuckers
